01 · The mission

AI made this game. Its job is to pay people.

Orion-81 exists to create paid work for graphic designers and software engineers who have lost work to AI.

The game starts out 100 % AI-generated. As it earns money, we hire designers to replace AI-made ships, art and interfaces piece by piece, credited by name, and engineers who move into AI-assisted development. Orion-81 is meant to be the first of many projects like it. The goal is to use AI to do some good for the people it affects.

02 · How it was made

100 % AI-generated.

Orion-81 is a completely AI-generated project. Every line of Orion-81's own code was written with Claude Code, directed by the founder. Every ship, map, texture and sound was generated by tools that Claude Code wrote. The founder decides what to build; Claude Code works out how. Where it started

Orion began on 28 September 2026 as a prototype on the open-source Quake III Arena engine (GPL), with ships from Elite Dangerous as its inspiration. On day two the game's code moved onto BuB, a new engine written with Claude Code; the game modules came across from the prototype and were renamed. Release 0.3.0 gave all 16 ship classes new hulls of their own. A licence-compliance review is checking whether any of the code is still derived from the GPL original, and whether the early ship designs are too close to their inspiration. An automated similarity scan of the code against the GPL prototype's source ran on 8 October 2026, and the expert review of its matches is in progress. Anything that is derived will be rewritten or redesigned.

Quake III Arena and Elite Dangerous are trademarks of their respective owners. Orion-81 is not affiliated with or endorsed by them.

        How the work is done

        Since 2026-10-03 (version 0.2.1 onward), a planner model (Claude Opus 5.5) writes the plan and acceptance criteria into a tracker. Executor models (Claude Sonnet 5.5) build in parallel, each on its own files. A planner reviews, executors fix, the checks run, and only then is a version frozen. Earlier versions (v0 to 0.2.0) were planned and built by Claude Code with a single model.

        Step 1 of 9 Founder decides what. The founder decides what to build, and reviews and accepts the result.

        07 · Questions

        Questions

        Why would an AI-made game help designers?

        Because its revenue is meant to pay them. The game starts fully AI-made so it can exist at all; paid designers then replace AI work, credited by name.

        Which parts will people replace, and how will I know?

        Ships, art, interface and more, as revenue allows. The ledger on this page lists every element and will show each replacement and its designer.

        Was anyone's work copied?

        The prototype was built on the open-source Quake III Arena engine (GPL) and took Elite Dangerous ships as inspiration. On day two the game's code moved onto BuB, a new engine written with Claude Code; the game modules came across from the prototype and were renamed. Release 0.3.0 gave all 16 ship classes new hulls. A licence-compliance review is checking whether any of the code is still derived from the GPL original and whether the early ship designs are too close to their inspiration. An automated similarity scan of the code against the GPL prototype's source ran on 8 October 2026, and the expert review of its matches is in progress. Anything that is derived will be rewritten or redesigned. Until the review is finished, we do not claim the answer is no.

        Is it free to play?

        That is the plan: free to play with optional in-game currency and cosmetics. It is not built yet.

        Will it be pay to win?

        Ships and upgrades are planned to be earnable by playing or buyable. How paid ships stay fair in player-versus-player is still being designed.

        Can I play online?

        Not yet. LAN multiplayer on the same network works today (since 0.2.0); online play is not built.

        When is it out, and on what?

        Windows PC. There is no release date and no price yet.
